# Fictional Submission: Student A

Students in my introductory course often complete the final research summary without explaining why their sources are credible. The instructions mention credible sources, but students see that phrase only once and receive feedback after the project is finished.

I would add a short source-evaluation checkpoint two weeks before the final submission. Students would select two planned sources and complete a worksheet addressing authorship, publication venue, supporting evidence, and relevance to the research question. I would provide brief feedback before students draft the final summary. This change directly addresses the current timing problem because students would practice the skill while they can still replace a weak source.

To evaluate the change, I would compare the source-quality portion of the final-project rubric with results from the previous course offering. I would also ask students whether the checkpoint helped them replace or refine a source. The comparison would show whether scores improved, while the survey would help explain how students used the feedback. A limitation is that the two course groups may differ, so I would treat the comparison as useful classroom evidence rather than proof that the checkpoint alone caused the change.
